# Laser Cutting

## Overivew
The NIACC Innovation Workspace has two carbon dioxide laser cutters available for our members to use: the Full Spectrum PS48 and the Epilog Fusion Pro.
This documentation includes everything you need to know about the lasers such as which machine to choose, what materials can be used, and how to perform maintenance on the machines.

## Machine Summary
### Full Spectrum PS48
This was the first laser purchased by the NIACC Innovation Workspace and it is a powerful machine. 
Boasting a 48 inch by 36 in cutting bed and a 150 W laser tube, this laser has the power to cut through 1/4 inch material with ease and is still delicate enough to engrave a photo onto cardboard.
Visit the Full Spectrum PS48 Profile for more details.

### Epilog Fusion Pro
The Epilog Fusion Pro is fast, reliable, and precise making it an exceptional machine to get started on.
With a 24 inch by 24 in cutting bed and a 60 W laser tube, this machine is a bit smaller and less powerful than the Full Spectrum, but its speed and precision make it the more refined of the two.
Visit the Epilog Fusion Pro Profile for more details.

## General Safety
The laser cutters in the NIACC Innovation Workspace are powerful tools and, consequently, they have several dangers.
- Always check the [Laser Materials](./Laser_Materials.md) list.
  - Some materials are toxic or messy and are banned. You always want to check.
  - Each laser can only cut through certian thicknesses, helps your project be successful.
- Always turn on the ventilation for the laser.
  - Both lasers have a ventilation fan, air assist, and must be connected to the fume extractor during operation.
  - **Note:** Currently the workspace has only a single fume extractor, so you may need to switch the extractor to the appropriate machine.
- Keep your hands free of the machine when it is moving!
  - The grantry of the laser can move quite quickly and can painfully crush fingers if they get caught while moving.
- Always check that the laser cabinet is fully closed before starting a project.
  - The laser cabinets are designed with special glass to keep the laser beam contained, even if it bounces after hitting the material.
  - Safety interlocks are in place to keep the laser from firing when the cabinet is open, but you should always make sure.
- Protect your eyes!
  - The glass of the laser cabinet will prevent the laser beam itself from escaping, which means we do not need to wear laser glasses while operating the laser cutters.
  - However, when the laser strikes the material, it can make a very bright spot in the visible light spectrum which can escape the cabinet.
  - Just like any other bright light source, this can do damage to your eyes, to avoid looking at this point for long (even though it is tempting).

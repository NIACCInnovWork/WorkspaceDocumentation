# Quickstart Guide
Whether you are using the Full Spectrum or Epilog Laser, review this Quickstart Guide for the general procedure to run a project in the laser cutter.

## Before Starting 
Before starting your project on the laser, go through the following sections. 
### Preparing Your Project
- [ ] Design File is the Proper Format
  - Cutting designs must be a vector graphic, such as a scaler vector graphic (\*.svg) or a drawing file (\*.dwg)
  - Cutting designs can be a raster graphic, such as a \*.jpg (Joint Photographic Experts Group) or portable network graphic (\*.png)
- [ ] Design Page is the Correct Size
  - We recommend setting your page size to match the material you will be cutting on. This way you can design with the layout in mind.
- [ ] Design Vectors are Grouped Appropriately
  - Occasionally the laser will struggle to interpret a design file. Often this can be solved by ungrouping everything to simplify the file as much as possible. 
- [ ] Colors are Set Correctly
  - The laser cutters use colors to distinquish between different settings within a particular project.
  - For example, you may wish to cut partway through cardboard to score it and then all the way through to cut out the design. 
- [ ] Remove any Guidelines or Invisible Elements
  - The laser doesn't necessarily understand whether an element is supposed to be a guideline or invisible, so make sure that you remove any extra elements in your design before sending it to the laser.
  - We recommend saving two versions of your design in this case, one with the guides, and a second copy that is intended to send to the laser.
     
### Choosing Your Material
- [ ] Visit the [Laser Materials](./Laser_Materials.md) to choose an approved laser material or check your preferred material.
  - Make sure to not only check the material, but the thickness the laser can handle and recommended settings. 
- [ ] Source your material
  - Make sure that you have some extra material to run setting tests. The recommended settings are a great starting place, but it is always a good idea to test the settings before doing the full project.
  - Consider the material's consistency. Some materials, such as wood, are not very uniform and this may impact how well the laser etch or cut works.
    - A knot in a piece of wood is typically denser than the wood around it, causing it to be difficult to etch and cut into.
    - A composite board made of multiple pieces of wood (like a bamboo cutting board) can have different densities in the different pieces also impacting the quality of the etch or cut. 

## Running Your Project
***Remember:*** You will probably want to do this process with some scrap material to test the settings prior to running the actual project.
### Before Powering on the Laser
- [ ] Check that the fume extractor is connected to the correct laser. If necessary, switch the connection to the correct laser.
- [ ] If you need to use the rotatary tool for your laser, connect it while the laser is turned off. 
### Power on the Laser
- [ ] Turn on the Laser
- [ ] Turn on the Air Assist
- [ ] Turn on the Exhaust Fan
- [ ] Turn on the Fume Extractor
### Load the Material into the Laser
- [ ] Open the laser cabinet and place your material on the cutting bed.
- [ ] Locate the material on the bed.
  - For the Epilog Fusion Edge, place the material in the upper left corner.
  - For the Full Spectrum PS48, use the Reference Markers **TODO: ADD REFERENCE MARKERS LINK** to add references (such as the corners) to show the location and orientation of your material.
- [ ] Level the laser off the top surface of the material. 
### Load the Design File
- [ ] Load your design file into the laser's control software.
- [ ] Choose the appropriate speed, power, dither, and other settings as are appropriate for your project and material.
- [ ] Check the perimeter of the design to preview the full size of the design on the laser.
### Run the Job
- [ ] Run the laser job.
- [ ] Keep an eye on the machine. You must monitor the laser for the entire project.
- [ ] Be on the lookout for the following:
  - Signs of excessive smoke, smoldering, melting, or fire.
  - Signs that a cut has gone the whole way through or failure to do so.
  - Signs that something has gone wrong: such as the wrong settings or the design being distorted in some way.
- [ ] If you want the job to be etched darker or cut deeper, you can run the job a second time without moving the material.
### Remove the Project
When removing your project, if you have any pieces that should have been cut out and you are not sure if they were, do the following:
- Try to pin down a corner or side of the material with your hands to keep it from moving on the cutting bed.
- Lift the other side or corner to see whether the cut piece comes free.
- If it does not, lay the piece back down and you can run the job again (without moving the piece) to cut the rest of the way through.

***Warning:*** This process always risks having a slight double-image if the material has shifted relative to the laser gantry when checking whether it cut all the way through.

***Expert Mode:*** It is possible to realign the material with the grantry on the Full Spectrum PS48 after the material has been fully removed from the laser. However, this is very trickey to do correctly and has a high likihood of creating a double-image, so it is not recommended for most users.

### Power Down the 
When you are done with your laser project for the day:
- [ ] Turn off the Laser
- [ ] Turn off the Air Assist
- [ ] Turn off the Exhaust Fan
- [ ] Turn off the Fume Extractor
- [ ] Close any design files you have open on the computer.
- [ ] Close the laser software.
- [ ] Report useage to the Workspace Staff.
